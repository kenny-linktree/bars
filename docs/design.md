# Bars design

This document records the agreed behavior of Bars and the reasons behind it. Change it together with any change to that behavior. The project vocabulary is defined in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md), and the decisions with lasting consequences are in [the ADRs](adr/).

## Agreed behavior

- Target macOS 14 or later, built and installed locally on each Mac.
- One account installs Bars on each Mac. Uninstall refuses an app owned by another account before stopping jobs or processes, unregistering the app or removing files. The check applies to the Python installer and both bootstrap removal paths, including `--keep-data`. A link at the app path is left in place.
- Use one large native WidgetKit overview for Claude, Codex, Cursor and Devin.
- Bars fill as usage increases. Show used and remaining amounts in the source's units. Cap visual fill at 100% while preserving over-limit amounts in text.
- Claude and Codex prefer their reported monthly budget or individual monthly credits. Without usable monthly amounts, they show the first available subscription quota in the contract's fixed order. The displayed allowance keeps its own label and units; the other general quota window appears as a mini bar. Model-specific quotas stay in details unless they are the only available primary allowance. Cursor shows individual included usage as its primary bar, with individual and team on-demand allowances as separately labeled mini bars beneath it.
- Every overview row, in the widget and the dropdown, follows one grammar: the provider name with its primary allowance label; used and left amounts; the bar with its pace marker; the pace line; a context line; then any secondary allowances. The context line gives every provider's reset: a time for periods up to a day, a weekday and time for a week, otherwise a date, marked `(est.)` when estimated. Without a reset it says `Shared team balance · no reset` for a team balance and `Reset not reported` otherwise. The compact widget shares one line between the pace and the reset to fit four providers. Currency amounts always show two decimals and the currency code; credits show whole numbers.
- Each proportional bar carries a pace marker at the share of the allowance period that had elapsed at the last successful fetch. Fill past the marker means usage is running ahead of an even spread. A pace line beneath the bar says `On pace · <n>% of period elapsed`, `Ahead of pace · runs out ~<date> at this rate` or `Used up · <n>% of period left`. A secondary allowance gains an `ahead of pace`, `used up early` or `previous period` tag when applicable. In details, an ahead-of-pace line also states the elapsed share of the period. Pace is computed from the metric's documented period length, its reset instant and its fetch time; it never changes the bar's color thresholds and is omitted for ended periods, balances and metrics without a known period.
- Devin shows its remaining on-demand balance. Its source supplies no genuine total or used amount, so the balance has no proportional bar. Daily and weekly quotas appear beneath it as labeled mini bars with their own reset times and pace tags, and as full bars in details; daily is omitted when the source hides it.
- Every provider keeps its own last successful values and fetch timestamp. Failed refreshes retain those values and show their age. Retained values from a known expired period are labeled as belonging to the previous period.
- Data age is consolidated. The widget and dropdown headers show when the latest successful fetch happened, as a time in the widget until that data is 30 minutes old and then as a relative age. A row shows its own age only when it deviates: it has never succeeded, its latest attempt failed, or its fetch is more than a minute from the latest. A failed or blocked row shows a status symbol and label before its age.
- The host app is a menu bar item without a Dock icon or standalone window. Clicking the status item opens a transient dropdown with the overview; a widget link opens the same dropdown on that provider's details. AppKit hosts the status item and popover because SwiftUI's `MenuBarExtra` cannot be opened programmatically, which the widget links require. The dropdown content remains SwiftUI. Dismissal is application-defined with a global outside-click monitor and Escape, because a transient popover closed itself as soon as the accessory app lost active status. A URL that launches the app is delivered before the status item exists, so the open is deferred until launch finishes.
- Clicking a row opens that provider's details, including all reported allowances, scope, reset times, status, manual refresh and a provider usage link. The details header combines the data age with the time of the last success and mentions a later unsuccessful attempt. Allowances are grouped under scope headings when their scopes differ; a single scope is stated in a footnote. A reset shared by every allowance is stated once above them. Each allowance lists its used, left and limit amounts and its reset in a two-column grid, omitting what the source does not report.
- A person can hide providers they do not use. Hiding adds the provider's ID to `disabled_providers` in `installation.json`; the collector then skips it and keeps its other fields unchanged, and the snapshot marks it `enabled: false`. The widget and the dropdown overview omit hidden rows. The dropdown footer names them in one tertiary line, `Hidden: Cursor, Devin`, with a Show button, or a Show menu for two or more. Every details view has a `Hide <Name>` button beside Refresh now and returns to the overview after hiding. A link to a hidden provider still opens its details, which offer `Show <Name>` instead of Refresh now. Hiding or showing refreshes that provider, so the collector republishes and the widget follows promptly. Consolidated freshness ignores hidden providers. With fewer than four rows the widget keeps rows top-aligned, with gaps capped at 14 pt.
- Missing or rejected logins become calls to action. When the collector reports `setup` after an attempt and nothing is retained, the row shows `Not set up` and the collector's message instead of a bar, and the dropdown row adds `Click for setup steps`. The details show setup steps in place of the metric cards, or before them when values are retained: an install link, the login command with a copy button and a prompt to choose Refresh now, followed by the login Bars reads and an offer to hide the provider. A `login_required` row keeps its status label and retained values, and the dropdown row adds `Click to see how to sign in`. Its details keep the status banner and metric cards and add a Sign in again box with the login command. The placeholder entry before the first collection has no attempt time and no call to action. Errors are unchanged.
- Reuse existing provider logins, including the Devin CLI. Do not depend on CodexBar. Preview renders use fixed synthetic data by default; reading the published snapshot requires an explicit `--variant live`.
- Use a short-lived Python collector and a Swift host app and widget. Collection operates independently of both native interfaces and continues after the host app quits.
- Target collection every 15 minutes while the Mac is awake, with login startup and recovery of missed calendar runs after sleep.

## Native build and storage

The Meter identity uses three left-aligned horizontal bars, shortest at the top and longest at the bottom. The app icon has a porcelain tile with green, orange and red fills in neutral grey tracks. The menu bar uses a monochrome template of the same ascending bars, drawn at 18 points so macOS can adapt it to the menu bar appearance. The mark is static and does not represent live usage.

The build uses Command Line Tools and ad-hoc signing, with no paid Apple Developer membership. Full Xcode remains an optional build path. The macOS deployment target is 14; installation and actual WidgetKit rendering were verified on the development machine running macOS 26.

An early direct executable probe could read an App Group, but the real WidgetKit-hosted extension could not. macOS rejected group access without authorized team signing. The implementation therefore uses an explicit, narrowly scoped storage arrangement:

1. Python writes the private staging snapshot at `~/Library/Application Support/Bars/snapshot.json`.
2. The native publisher validates bounded input, writes a mode-0600 temporary file, flushes it and atomically replaces `widget-snapshot.json` in the same directory.
3. The sandboxed widget receives a home-relative read-only exception for exactly that published file. It receives no credential access or write access.

Native code resolves the account's actual home rather than the widget's sandbox home. Publication failures preserve the previous file and return an error; there is no automatic storage fallback. Actual system-hosted rendering verified this arrangement. A future distribution should reassess storage and signing. See [ADR 0004](adr/0004-publish-one-file-for-the-personal-widget.md).

## Collection and display lifecycle

Scheduled refreshes skip when another run holds the lock. Explicit dropdown refreshes and hide/show requests use a bounded wait of 150 seconds and read installation settings only after acquiring the lock. Every successful collection with a saved snapshot attempts final publication, even when all providers are hidden and the snapshot did not change. This lets publication recover after a transient failure without changing fetch timestamps.

The dropdown creates its hosted view on first opening and retains it for subsequent openings. While open, one timer reads the snapshot about every five seconds and advances display time about once a minute. Closing stops that timer; reopening reloads the snapshot and clock immediately. Successful reads still validate the complete file, but unchanged snapshots and error messages do not notify SwiftUI to redraw. Ongoing collection and provider selection stay in the model when the dropdown closes.

The per-user LaunchAgent runs at login and on the quarter hour. Missed calendar events resume after wake. The refresh runner locks each run, starts the collector with a bounded lifetime, publishes completed provider results as they arrive, and requests a WidgetKit timeline reload at completion. A `SIGTERM` request to the runner stops its collector before releasing the refresh lock, including a request received during child-process startup. Provider workers have independent deadlines and retain their own last successful state after failures. No collector process stays resident between runs.

Both native interfaces read the same published file. The dropdown re-reads it every five seconds while open and on every open, and the widget's timeline is regenerated when the publisher requests a reload. WidgetKit decides whether a reload request counts against the widget's daily budget; logs on the development machine showed a mix of exempt and budgeted reloads for Bars' own requests, and the widget's 15-minute fallback policy is always budgeted.

The widget extension is a separate long-lived process that macOS keeps alive, suspended, between reloads. Replacing the bundle on disk does not replace that process, and it kept producing timelines from the old binary for hours after an install, so the widget and the dropdown showed different code. The installer therefore ends the running widget extension and Bars app processes after the swap, requests a timeline reload through `Bars --reload-widgets`, and relaunches the menu bar app, all while holding the refresh lock so no publication races the restart.

WidgetKit controls display scheduling. A 15-minute collection interval cannot guarantee a 15-minute display interval. Relative timestamp text continues to age between timeline entries; entries also schedule stale and allowance-expiry changes. See [Apple's refresh documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date/).

The versioned [snapshot contract](snapshot-contract.md) distinguishes unknown limits, zero limits, used amounts and remaining balances. The optional `period` field records each allowance's documented length so the reader can derive pace; Codex's session window has no period because its length is not reported. The collector selects primary allowances using the explicit provider priorities in the contract. Selection uses only the current successful response, not a guessed plan name or retained allowance from another response. Missing windows can change the selected label on the next successful refresh; failed or malformed responses retain the previous selection and values. Each allowance retains its identity rather than being merged with another allowance that happens to have equal units or limits.

## Provider semantics

Claude's currency and exponent come from its reported money fields. Its budget scope remains unknown because the response does not establish whether it is personal or organizational. Codex uses its individual credit cap. Cursor reports USD cents and exposes separate individual included, individual on-demand and team on-demand allowances.

Devin authenticates directly with the existing CLI credential and discovers its organization through `devin cloud drs whoami`. The quota response supplies daily/weekly percentages, reset times, a daily visibility flag and the remaining on-demand balance. The balance is a shared team balance in USD; its total, used amount and reset are unknown. Quota scope remains unknown. No monthly reset is invented. [Devin's billing documentation](https://docs.devin.ai/admin/billing/self-serve#on-demand-credits) explains that Teams on-demand credits are shared and purchased credits do not expire.

The earlier Übersicht widget that Bars replaced treated Devin's balance as spend and labeled Codex's individual cap as workspace credits. Bars corrects those interpretations. Credentials, account identifiers and raw response bodies are excluded from snapshots and logs. See the [collector documentation](../collector/README.md) for authentication paths, parser behavior and resource measurements.

## Verification

The collector, scheduler/publisher boundary and Swift presentation/storage checks passed after implementation and independent review. Tests cover independent failures, retained timestamps, provider scope, missing limits, overage, previous periods, process cleanup, install rollback and rejected publication input. The installed widget was verified under the real macOS widget host with successful data from all four providers. Synthetic light and dark previews also checked row layout and error states.

Manual refresh of all providers and a Devin-only refresh were verified, including unchanged timestamps for the other providers. Background refresh also succeeded with the host app fully closed. The collector supports Python 3.9 or later, so Apple's Command Line Tools interpreter at `/usr/bin/python3` is sufficient; installation was verified on the development machine with both it and Homebrew's CPython 3.14.

The calendar schedule implements login startup and wake recovery, but actual logout/login and physical sleep/wake cycles were not tested. The full-Xcode build path was not tested either. Commands are in the [README](../README.md); [verification.md](verification.md) records the results and remaining UI limitations.

## History

- 2026-10-01: Initial design and implementation, replacing an earlier Übersicht AI usage widget. The archived Übersicht code informed the provider endpoints, not the architecture. The collector, publisher and native presentation received independent reviews; the menu bar dropdown replaced the original standalone window the same day.
- 2026-10-02: An information-architecture pass over the overview, widget and details introduced the shared row grammar, secondary allowance mini bars, consolidated data age, resets on every overview row and the details grid.
- 2026-10-02: Calls to action for setup and login, and hiding unused providers, were added because many people use only some of the four providers. The overview row grammar is unchanged for providers with data.
